Product and UI review · AndroidUsageControl v0.2

Usage Control Redesign

The app captures notifications and auto-dismisses them. It does not yet change how the phone feels. This review proposes a theme with a point of view, a three-tab shell, and a notification model built on lanes, sender screening, batched delivery and a forgiving archive.

3 Nov 2026Kotlin · Compose · Material 3 1.2 → 1.5 ExpressiveFull text: .planning/research/DESIGN-REVIEW.md

Five findings

  1. The app has no identity.Dynamic colour is on, the fallback is stock Material green, and every tab is the same top bar over a list of cards. An app whose job is colour-coding notifications cannot let the wallpaper choose its colours. Go dark-first, static palette, one motif: the notification LED.
  2. Five tabs hide one idea.Notifications, Screen Time, Controls, Insights and Settings all describe the same thing from different angles: every app gets a policy. Three tabs (Now, Apps, Modes) say that out loud. Rules and Insights fold into a per-app profile.
  3. The notification feature is a log.The listener stores title and text and executes one action, dismiss. Mute and snooze exist only as enum values. No icons, channels, people, actions or intents are captured, and dismissals are matched by timestamp instead of key.
  4. Lanes are the product.A lane is a named bucket with a colour, a delivery policy (Instant, Batched, Quiet, Digest, Blocked), a decay rule and breakthrough keywords. New senders are screened once and routed forever. Everything removed is recoverable. No Android app combines content bucketing, keyword breakthrough, sender screening and deferral to breakpoints.
  5. The SemPKM link does not exist and the privacy rule forbids it.No INTERNET permission; SemPKM's own Expo app already does the job. Proposal: an opt-in bridge module speaking the existing context API, sending context only, never content, plus two new fields: focus mode and notification pressure.

1 · What exists today

The Notifications tab, as built. Three things are visible at once: the dashboard pushes the feed below the fold, every card looks identical, and there is nothing to do with a notification except expand it.

Screen by screen

  • Theme. dynamicColor = true, so on Android 12+ the app wears the wallpaper. Material defaults for type and shape. Material 3 is pinned at 1.2.0, before Expressive.
  • Navigation. Five tabs plus a hidden Rules screen reached from three of them. Insights and the Notifications dashboard draw the same Total / Dismissed / Opened triplet and the same trend line twice.
  • Notifications. No app icon, no colour, no time sections, no search (the DAO has a search() the UI never calls), no swipe. Expanding a card offers "Auto-dismiss this app" and nothing else.
  • Controls. The name says nothing; these are modes. The context card is the most interesting object in the app and it is buried on tab three.
  • Insights. Suggestions are phrased as rules ("Auto-dismiss X") with a confidence bar. Users do not think in rules; they think "this app is noise".
  • Onboarding. The permission walkthrough is good. The welcome screen is a paragraph with nothing to look at.

Listener defects that block everything below

  • Removals are matched by postTime ± 1 s, not by sbn.key. Updates to the same key insert new rows, inflating every count.
  • No small or large icon, colour, picture, MessagingStyle messages, channel id, importance, conversation flag, actions, RemoteInputs or contentIntent are stored. The feed cannot show an icon; a restored notification cannot open or reply.
  • Ongoing notifications (media, foreground services, progress) are logged as events and pollute history and stats.
  • No getActiveNotifications() sync on connect, so after a restart the app does not know what is in the shade.

2 · Theme: the LED is back

The first Android notification UI was a coloured LED blinking in the dark. Glanceable, colour-coded, silent. One glowing dot per lane on a near-black ground gives the app an icon, a reason to be dark-first, and an indicator that works in the shade, the status bar, a widget and the feed.

Decisions

  • Dynamic colour off by default. Lane colours are semantic. Offer "Match wallpaper" as an opt-in that recolours only the accent.
  • Dark-first, light-complete. Near-black with a cool ink bias and four stepped surfaces. The light theme is warm paper with the same hues at lower chroma, not an inversion. This page is rendered in that palette; switch your theme to compare.
  • One accent, amber. The "lamp is on" colour, used for primary actions, the active mode and the batch countdown. Good / warn / critical stay separate from the accent.
  • Type. Manrope for UI, DM Mono for times, counts and package names, tabular numerals wherever digits align. Both ship as single variable TTFs.
  • Shape. Rows and sheets at 12 dp, chips and the LED pill fully round, one large expressive element per screen. Cards only for the current batch and for suggestions; rows for everything else.
  • Material 3 Expressive. Upgrade to material3 1.5.0-beta02 (Expressive is not in 1.4.0 stable) and wrap the app in MaterialExpressiveTheme with the expressive motion scheme: ButtonGroup for the policy picker, SplitButton on the batch card, FloatingToolbar for multi-select in the archive, flexible top app bars on profiles, LoadingIndicator while the listener syncs. Build hierarchy with surface containers, not tonal elevation; Material advises against pure black behind content, so the ground is near-black with an optional true-black setting.

3 · Three tabs, not five

Everything in the app is per app, and everything is a policy. The shell should say so.

Notifications feed + dashboard
Screen Time stats + limits
Controls presets + context
Insights suggestions + stats
Settings permissions, data
Rules hidden, per pillar
→
Now stream, batch, undo, search
Apps one profile per app and person
Modes Night, Meeting, Deep Work, Home
Settings gear in the Now top bar
  • Now replaces the Notifications tab, the dashboard and history.
  • Apps replaces Rules, Insights and screen-time limits. A profile holds the lane, the policy, the stats, the channels, the people, the limit and suggestions for that app.
  • Modes replaces Controls and screen-time blocking. A mode bundles sound, DND, lock timeout, which lanes are instant and which apps are blocked, and shows the live context strip.

5 · The notification model

Four parts: lanes, screening, delivery, memory. Each replaces something the app does today with something the user can name.

ArrivesListener stores key, channel, person, icons, actions, intent.
Known sender?App + channel, or app + person. If not: the Screener. Until screened, Android category and importance pick a provisional lane.
LaneColour, policy, decay, sound, breakthrough keywords and people.
PolicyInstant passes through untouched. The rest are mirrored under the app's own channels.
ArchiveEvery removal, with its reason. Undo, restore, search, keyword pages, digest.

Policies

InstantOriginal left alone. People, Money, breakthroughs.
BatchedHeld and delivered at set times, or at a breakpoint when the phone is in use.
QuietDelivered now, silently, grouped under the lane.
DigestNever shown alone. One line in the next batch summary.
BlockedCancelled and archived. Still searchable, still restorable.

Decay instead of dismiss

"Auto-dismiss" reads as deletion. Decay reads as intent: promotions expire at the next batch, delivery updates expire when the app is opened, codes expire after ten minutes. The archive keeps all of it.

Screening: decide once, route forever

The first time a new sender appears, nothing is auto-dismissed. One quiet notification asks where it goes; one tap routes that sender forever and re-files its history. This is HEY's Screener for notifications, and it replaces most of the suggestion engine. The heuristics stay, but as re-screening prompts: "You dismissed 40 of 42 from Zalando in under 3 s. Move to Noise?"

Delivery rules

  • Two or three batches a day per lane by default. The Fitz et al. trial found three predictable batches beat hourly batching and beat silence; silence raised anxiety.
  • Bounded deferral. When the phone is unlocked and in use, a batched delivery waits for screen-off, an app switch, or a maximum delay, whichever comes first. A third of notifications arrive mid-task.
  • Cooldown per sender. One alert per N minutes; the rest fold into the bundle row.
  • Modes override lanes. Deep Work makes Work instant; Night demotes everything but VIP People. Held batches deliver when the mode ends.
  • Snooze to a clock time, not a duration.
  • Breakthrough. Keywords ("code", "urgent", "delivered"), people and channels bypass any policy.

Memory: undo, archive, digest

  • The 10-second undo chip after a swipe, a "Recently dismissed" row, and "Restore batch" on expired bundles. Restored notifications are working mirrors: they open and reply.
  • The archive is the history tab the app already half has: search across title, text, sender, lane, plus saved searches ("Invoice", "OTP") as keyword pages that can become breakthrough rules in one tap.
  • The digest is template text generated on-device with no model: "3 deliveries (DHL, Amazon), 12 promotions expired, 2 messages from Anna held in Work." On-device summarisation can come later and must be labelled.

The SemPKM bridge

Phone to SemPKM: POST /api/context/update with zone, activity, time period, calendar event and busy flag, device id, plus two fields SemPKM does not have yet: focus_mode and notification_pressure. SemPKM to phone: register as a device and receive persona switches and rule notifications into a SemPKM lane; a persona switch can activate a mode. The bridge is its own Gradle module, the only one holding INTERNET, off until an instance URL and key are entered, and it never sends notification content. PROJECT.md's "no internet permission" rule needs rewriting to "nothing leaves the device except context, to your own instance".

7 · What a listener can do

The whole model stands on one technical commitment: for non-Instant lanes, cancel the original and re-post a mirror under the app's own channels. Here is what the platform allows.

Read everythingRanking gives channel, importance, conversation flag and shortcut; extras give messages, people, picture, inbox lines; small and large icons load cross-package; actions carry RemoteInputs; contentIntent is readable.
Cancel, snooze, synccancelNotification, snoozeNotification(key, ms) with the system re-posting it, getActiveNotifications on connect, setNotificationsShown. A listener cancel is silently ignored for ongoing and foreground-service notifications, so the Live lane always passes through.
Mirror a notificationNotification.Builder.recoverBuilder keeps the intent, actions, RemoteInputs, messaging content and large icon. Set our channel, lane colour and group, the original app icon as large icon and its label as sub-text. Cancel the original first.
Open from a mirrorA tap on the mirror is sent by SystemUI, exempt from background-launch rules. From our own Activity, targeting 34+, pass ActivityOptions with background-start allowed. From a service it is blocked.
Modes with device effectsAndroid 15 AutomaticZenRule with type, icon, trigger text and ZenDeviceEffects (grayscale, dim wallpaper). Needs DND access, which the app already asks for. Modes show up in system Settings.
Reply from a mirrorWorks when the app made its reply intent mutable, as Google's guidance says it should. Immutable ones deliver an empty reply. Test per messaging app; fall back to opening the conversation.
Undo a dismissalNothing restores the original. Snooze instead of cancel when the user is likely to want it back; otherwise restore a mirror from the stored parcel.
Colour, group, order, silenceOnly on our mirrors. The header shows our app name. Making an original silent after the fact is impossible; cancel and re-post on a low-importance channel.
Re-rank or re-classify the shadeNotificationAssistantService is system-only, one per device. Pixel's Notification Organizer and Gemini summaries cannot be hooked. On Android 16 QPR1 a listener probably sees bundled items under reserved channel ids, usable as lane hints.
Notification cooldownNo developer API. Approximate per sender with our own cooldown; the ranking says when the system last alerted.

To test on a device before Phase E: snoozing foreground-service notifications, channel ids of bundled notifications on Pixel, and reply mutability across WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, Slack and Gmail. The full API table is section 4 of the written review in the repository.

9 · Decisions for the owner

  1. Rename or keep "Usage Control". The mocks use "Doorman" as a placeholder.
  2. Accept the privacy rewrite for the SemPKM bridge, or keep this app offline and let the Expo app remain the Android context provider.
  3. Accept the mirroring trade-off for non-Instant lanes before Phase E.
  4. Default lanes and batch times for first run.
